# Direction

What this site is for, and the structure that follows from it. Written
October 2026. Revisit at every checkpoint (see *How it evolves*).

---

## The short version

This is not a portfolio and not a declaration that I'm a UX designer.

It's a **commitment device**: a public, dated record of whether I'm
actually exploring design — and what I learn while I do. It is written for
me first. Visitors are welcome, but the site isn't trying to convince them
of anything.

> Figuring out how things should work — and whether designing them is
> what I want to do. Exploring since October 2026.

---

## What I know so far (the evidence, not the story)

- UX and front-end web development came easily in poly; algebraic maths
  did not.
- I don't enjoy code for its own sake. I do enjoy the part where
  decisions become something you can see and use.
- **The one project I finished had a real client** — the property agent
  site. Everything I worked on alone is half done.
- What I enjoyed on that project was working out what was needed, and the
  tension between the client's requirements and my own judgement.
- Website Factory — a multi-agent pipeline to generate property agent
  sites — was an idea I only lightly started. The site I did finish, I
  made by going back and forth with Claude.

Keep this list factual. Add to it at checkpoints. Don't let it turn into a
flattering narrative.

### On the label

"UX design" is probably too narrow. What I described is closer to
**product / web design**: interpreting a need, making judgement calls,
and making the result real on the web. But for the next two years the
audience is me, so **the site carries no label** — it carries a question
instead.

## Structure

```
Home
├── Projects        one list, each with a status: Idea · In progress · Parked · Done
│   └── /projects/[slug]
├── Log             dated entries: note · experiment · update · checkpoint (+ tags, e.g. "ai")
│   └── /log/[slug]
└── About           a "now" page: why, what I'm exploring, what I'm unsure of
```

Navigation: **Projects · Log · About**. The name links home. Contact is an
email address at the bottom of the page, not a call to action.

### Two kinds of content, and the test for each

| | Test | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Project** | Can I name *who it was for* and *what they needed*? | The property agent site (Done). Website Factory (Idea). The old KIV items (Idea). |
| **Log entry** | If I can only name what I'm *curious about*, it's an experiment. Anything dated goes here. | The dithering study. The Lab experiments on Claude. Notes. Checkpoints. |

The Log is deliberately one flexible stream. I don't yet know what I'll
publish, so new formats become new **kinds or tags**, not new sections.

### Homepage

1. One sentence plus "exploring since".
2. **Currently exploring** — two or three open questions.
3. **Latest from the log** — the five most recent entries, dated. The date
   of the newest one is visible, which is the point.
4. **Projects** — compact list with statuses.
5. Email.

### A project write-up (only for projects that earned one)

Not the conventional UX case study template. Structured around the part I
actually enjoyed:

1. **The need** — who it was for and what they actually needed.
2. **Their requirements** — what the client asked for.
3. **Where my judgement differed** — and what I did about it.
4. **Decisions and trade-offs.**
5. **What I'd change.**

Ideas and parked projects get a few honest lines instead: what I was
trying to figure out, how far it got, why it stopped.

### About

A "now" page, not a bio: why I started, what I'm exploring, the questions
I'm holding — including *"is design actually it?"* — one or two lines of
background, and an email.

## Deliberately left out

- Role titles of any kind, especially rotating ones.
- Resume / CV, "services", "hire me", testimonials, "open to projects".
- Tool and stack lists, skill bars, logos.
- Metrics and big numbers.
- Boilerplate process diagrams.
- Separate sections for writing, AI, or experiments — they're tags.
- Newsletter, view counts, "featured" badges.
- Anything framed around speed or volume of output.

## How it evolves

**A floor, not a schedule.** At least one log entry a month, of any kind —
three lines is fine. Every three months, a **checkpoint** entry:

- Is this still the direction?
- What surprised me?
- What am I dropping?

The checkpoints are the commitment device.

**Grow the structure on triggers, not dates:**

- A tag shows up three or more times with a real thread running through it
  → it may get its own page. Not before.
- AI entries shift from *"what is this?"* to *"how I used it"* → retire the
  `ai` tag; AI just appears inside normal entries. (That's the stated goal:
  explore AI until I can say I use it as a tool.)
- I take on a project for a real person → it becomes the second full
  write-up. Look for one; that's when I finish things.
- Internship season (~2028) → add a curated "Selected work" page on top.
  Don't restructure — the Log is the evidence.
- A checkpoint concludes design isn't it → the site still works. Change the
  question on the homepage; keep everything else.
